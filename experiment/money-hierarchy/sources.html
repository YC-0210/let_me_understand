01 · Find the money

Printed pp. 7–8. Gold → currency → deposits → securities is a historical teaching model. The money/credit boundary depends on the settlement problem. Modern reserve balances are distinct from notes and coins; modern fiat currency generally does not promise gold.

02 · Trace the promise

Printed p. 9. Matching claims cancel within a chosen consolidation boundary. Currency is outside the private sector but inside a boundary containing its issuer. Gold has no issuer’s liability. Our 100-unit entries are an illustrative excerpt, not complete balance sheets; securities, loans, equity and other entries are omitted. Consolidation does not repay debts.

03 · Make room to trade

Printed pp. 11–12. Issuing a credible, accepted promise can relax today’s payment constraint. The issuer cannot create a higher layer’s settlement asset unilaterally. More credit is not automatically more settlement money.

04 · Put trust under stress

Printed pp. 10–12. Separate credit quantity from credit’s moneyness. Counts and distances in the diagram are illustrative, not data, estimated elasticities or a calibrated crisis model.

05 · Connect the layers

Printed pp. 12–13. Dealers, banks and central banks connect layers through security prices/interest rates, par, and the gold exchange rate in the historical example. Supporting conversion can conceal qualitative differences until stress makes them visible.

06 · Manage the tension

Printed pp. 13–14. Policy can influence discipline and elasticity; longer rates need not follow the overnight rate mechanically. The reserve-loan diagram is a marked modern schematic extension. Liquidity support adds a repayment obligation and need not cure insolvency.
