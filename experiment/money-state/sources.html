Historical checks

  • Formal gold redemption resumed on 1 January 1879; late-1878 market parity is a separate milestone. Federal Reserve research, IFDP 898.
  • The 1913 Act, the 1914 opening, eligible-paper rediscounts, 1916 secured advances, and the development of coordinated open-market operations belong to different moments. The Fed’s Formative Years.
  • World War I support included marketing war bonds and preferential lending secured by government obligations. The lesson does not equate that support with only outright securities purchases. The Fed’s Role During WWI.

How to read the accounts

These are changes in simplified balance sheets, not complete historical accounts. Entries are revealed in teaching order, while their accounting effects belong to the same transaction. $100 is an illustrative unit. Interest, fees, profit and detailed statutory issuance limits are omitted unless explicitly introduced. Bond and security sales use carrying value. The rediscount example sells a bill carried at $99, with a $100 face value, for $99; no unrecorded sale gain or loss is assumed.

The Fed is consolidated for the diagrams. The Board is not drawn as a separate bank lending to Reserve Banks. A member bank’s own notes are not its lawful-money reserve. National note issue was constrained, not an absolutely fixed quantity of all money. Government securities in WWI are not called “Treasury bills.”

Additional lecture context

Young’s 1924 argument places money inside banking and finance, rather than treating it only as a convenient replacement for barter. His criticisms of rigid currency rules and unrestrained speculation help explain the hybrid conclusion. His gold-standard commitments are historical positions, not assumptions about today’s monetary system.

The opening FT segment discusses the Fed’s enlarged balance sheet around the financial crisis. It supplies a modern connection: the asset acquired and liability issued matter. This experiment concentrates on the three historical stories; it does not reproduce that clip’s full QE chart or treat its commentary as current policy.
