# Economics teaching references

First experiment: [Natural hierarchy of money](../../experiment/money-hierarchy/index.html).
Read the shared [principles](../principles.json) first. These cards are design hypotheses,
not a ranking of economists or a claim of tested learning gains.

## Approved scope versus proposed concepts

The user selected Mehrling's chapter 2 as the primary Source. Its seven proposed Concepts
are in the lesson manifest; the app still requires explicit concept-list approval and
understanding marks. Neither viewing nor answering a reflection automatically marks learning.

## References reviewed

- **Perry Mehrling**, user-supplied Fall 2016 course notes, printed pp. 7–14 (PDF pp. 8–15):
  text and diagrams reviewed. Hierarchy, paired entries, boundaries, dynamics, elasticity,
  market makers and policy are the main line. The public [author's lecture page](https://sites.bu.edu/perry/lectures/mb-lectures/section-1/2-the-natural-hierarchy-of-money/)
  provides a companion route; its linked 2009 notes are an earlier edition.
- **Bank of England**, [Money in the modern economy: an introduction](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/quarterly-bulletin/2014/q1/money-in-the-modern-economy-an-introduction):
  the IOU account and paired sector entries inform the asset/liability explanation. Currency,
  deposits and reserves retain distinct meanings. The 2014 data are not used as current figures.
- **CORE Econ**, [Unit 6 teaching guide](https://www.core-econ.org/te2-0-macro-guide/):
  balance-sheet exercises motivate concrete questions before abstract terminology.
  [Inflation and central-bank policy choices](https://coreecon.github.io/voici/render/inflation.html)
  supplies a reference for comparing a benchmark with changed cases and explaining an outcome.
  Its interactive controls and page were inspected, including the benchmark toggle.
  The [Skyscrapers time-lapse](https://www.core-econ.org/visualisations/) was also played and
  visually inspected as an animation reference: changes remain anchored in a consistent chart
  frame. We retain that continuity principle, without copying its 3-D encoding or income data. Its inflation equations, forecasts, text
  and artwork are not copied into this hierarchy lesson.

## Teaching cards

| Card | Use when | Teaching move | Avoid | Reader question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ECON01 · Change the viewpoint | “Money” sounds like a permanent object category | Hold the instrument fixed; change whose payment must settle | A universal money/credit dividing line | What is money to the holder but credit to the issuer? |
| ECON02 · Find the counterpart | A new asset looks like free wealth | Show what the holder gains beside what the issuer owes | Duplicating one claim into two piles of wealth | Who must fulfil this claim? |
| ECON03 · Draw the boundary | Inside/outside seems intrinsic | Include or exclude the issuer explicitly | Treating consolidation as repayment | What changed: the contract or the boundary? |
| ECON04 · Separate dimensions | Credit quantity and quality are conflated | Change one variable while holding the other fixed | Unlabelled simulated data | Can the same number of promises become harder to use? |
| ECON05 · Explain a changed case | A learner can repeat terms but not use them | Ask a prediction; offer a voluntary explanation reveal | Scoring recall as mastery | Does 1:1 conversion make two instruments identical? |

## Animation adaptations

Stable spatial anchors preserve instrument identity. Brackets show an accounting boundary;
matching signed entries show a claim's two sides. Fixed-size tokens travel at 180 SVG units/s
for accepted IOUs and reserve support; they do not multiply or shrink in transit. Quantity is
encoded by claim count. Qualitative convertibility is encoded by distance, explicitly labelled
as schematic, not data. The six library players share the exact lesson renderer.
